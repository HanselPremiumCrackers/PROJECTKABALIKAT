Mathematical Applications in Community Disaster Management


1. Population Per Evacuation Center

Problem: A municipality with 50,000 residents needs to distribute people across evacuation centers with a capacity of 500 people each.

Mathematical Model: Number of Centers = Total Population ÷ Center Capacity

Solution: 50,000 ÷ 500 = 100 evacuation centers needed

2. Emergency Supply Computation

Problem: Each evacuee needs 3 liters of water per day and 2 meals per day. For 10,000 people for 5 days, calculate total supplies needed.

Mathematical Model:

  • Total Water = Population × Water per person × Number of days
  • Total Meals = Population × Meals per person × Number of days

Solution:

  • Total Water = 10,000 × 3 × 5 = 150,000 liters
  • Total Meals = 10,000 × 2 × 5 = 100,000 meals

3. Evacuation Scheduling Patterns & Sequences

Problem: An evacuation must process people in waves. If the first wave evacuates 500 people, and each subsequent wave increases by 150 people, what is the sequence for 5 waves?

Mathematical Model (Arithmetic Sequence): aₙ = a₁ + (n-1)d

Where: a₁ = 500 (first wave), d = 150 (common difference), n = wave number

Evacuation Schedule:

  • Wave 1: 500 people
  • Wave 2: 650 people (500 + 150)
  • Wave 3: 800 people (650 + 150)
  • Wave 4: 950 people (800 + 150)
  • Wave 5: 1,100 people (950 + 150)
  • Total Evacuated: 4,000 people

4. Resource Distribution Optimization

Problem: A community has 1,000 emergency kits to distribute among 5 evacuation centers. How many kits per center for equal distribution?

Mathematical Model: Kits per Center = Total Kits ÷ Number of Centers

Solution: 1,000 ÷ 5 = 200 emergency kits per center
