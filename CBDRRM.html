1. CBDRRM Overview

What is CBDRRM?

The Comprehensive Barangay Disaster Risk Reduction and Management (CBDRRM) is a systematic approach to reducing vulnerabilities and risks in the barangay. It integrates disaster risk reduction into development planning and promotes resilience at the community level.

Key Components:

  • Risk Assessment and Mapping
  • Preparedness Planning
  • Early Warning Systems
  • Emergency Response Coordination
  • Recovery and Rehabilitation
  • Community Education and Awareness

2. Preparedness Plan

Before Disaster Strikes:

  • Community Training: Regular drills and training for residents on evacuation procedures
  • Resource Stockpiling: Emergency supplies including water, food, medicines, and first aid kits
  • Infrastructure Assessment: Identifying safe zones and evacuation routes
  • Communication Systems: Establishing alert and notification systems
  • Community Volunteers: Training barangay disaster volunteers for quick response
  • Coordination with Agencies: Partnership with PNP, BFP, and health services

5. Preparedness Checklist

Family Emergency Kit Should Include:

  • ☐ 2-week supply of non-perishable food
  • ☐ 1 gallon water per person per day (2-week supply)
  • ☐ First aid kit and essential medicines
  • ☐ Flashlight and extra batteries
  • ☐ Battery-powered or hand-crank radio
  • ☐ Copies of important documents (ID, insurance papers)
  • ☐ Cash and credit cards
  • ☐ Change of clothes and sturdy shoes
  • ☐ Toiletries and personal hygiene items
  • ☐ Infant formula and diapers (if applicable)
  • ☐ Pet supplies and carriers
  • ☐ Photos of family members for identification

Community Preparedness Tasks:

  • ☐ Conduct community hazard mapping quarterly
  • ☐ Hold monthly barangay disaster preparedness meetings
  • ☐ Conduct evacuation drills every 3 months
  • ☐ Update and maintain evacuation centers
  • ☐ Train volunteers monthly on first aid and rescue
  • ☐ Inspect and maintain early warning systems
  • ☐ Distribute preparedness materials to residents
  • ☐ Establish communication hotlines

7. Electromagnetic Waves and Disaster Communication

Role of Electromagnetic Waves in Emergency Systems:

Electromagnetic waves are essential for disaster warning systems, including radio signals, cell phone networks, and emergency broadcast systems that alert communities to incoming threats.

Key Communication Channels:

  • Radio Waves (3 kHz - 300 GHz): Used for emergency broadcasts and two-way radio communications
  • Microwave Signals: Utilized for cellular networks and data transmission during emergencies
  • Infrared Radiation: Used in thermal imaging for rescue operations and damage assessment
  • Visible Light: Emergency signal lights and visual warning systems

9. Earthquake Preparedness: Before, During, and After

BEFORE AN EARTHQUAKE:

  • Secure Heavy Objects: Anchor bookcases, cabinets, and appliances to walls to prevent them from falling
  • Know Safe Spots: Identify sturdy tables, desks, and interior walls in each room
  • Plan Escape Routes: Know where exits are and how to leave your home safely
  • Store Supplies: Keep emergency kits in accessible locations - at home, work, and in your vehicle
  • Practice Drills: Conduct earthquake drills with family members regularly
  • Know Shut-offs: Learn how to turn off gas, water, and electricity at main switches
  • Share Plans: Establish a family communication plan and meeting point
  • Update Documents: Keep copies of important documents in waterproof containers

DURING AN EARTHQUAKE:

  • DROP, COVER, AND HOLD ON: This is the most important action
    • DROP to hands and knees immediately
    • COVER your head and neck under a sturdy table or desk
    • HOLD ON until shaking stops
  • Stay Inside: Do not run outside; falling debris outside is more dangerous
  • Avoid Doorways: Contrary to old advice, doorways are not safer than under sturdy tables
  • Stay Away from Windows: Glass can shatter and cause severe injuries
  • In a Car: Pull over safely, stay inside with seatbelt on, avoid overpasses and power lines
  • Outdoors: Move away from buildings, trees, and power lines
  • Near Coast: Move to higher ground if you feel strong shaking (tsunami risk)
  • Stay Calm: Keep yourself and others calm; panic can lead to poor decisions

AFTER AN EARTHQUAKE:

  • Check for Injuries: Provide first aid to those in need; call emergency services if necessary
  • Inspect Your Home: Check for gas leaks, electrical damage, and structural damage
  • Shut Off Utilities: Turn off gas if you smell gas; turn off electricity if wires are exposed
  • Exit Safely: Wear sturdy shoes to protect from broken glass; be careful of debris
  • Aftershocks: Expect aftershocks; be prepared to DROP, COVER, AND HOLD ON again
  • Stay Informed: Listen to local news and official alerts for information and instructions
  • Help Neighbors: Check on neighbors, especially elderly or disabled individuals
  • Document Damage: Take photos for insurance purposes
  • Do Not Use Elevators: Use stairs to evacuate buildings
  • Reunite Family: Use your established communication plan to connect with family members
