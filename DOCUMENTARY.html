Dr. José Rizal’s 19th-century novel El Filibusterismo serves as a stark mirror to contemporary society, demonstrating that the systemic ills of the Spanish colonial era remain deeply entrenched today. The novel's central themes of political corruption, abuse of power, and government inefficiency directly parallel modern political dynasties and bureaucratic red tape. Just as the colonial government disregarded the needs of ordinary citizens, modern societies continue to grapple with leaders who prioritize self-preservation and wealth accumulation over public service. The systemic greed depicted through characters like the friars and corrupt officials echoes perfectly in today's multi-million dollar corruption scandals and the ongoing marginalization of the working class.

Furthermore, the novel’s core conflict—Simoun’s radical attempt to trigger a violent revolution born out of despair—resonates deeply with modern global struggles against inequality. Rizal highlights how educational discrimination, judicial injustice, and economic oppression push the marginalized to the brink of desperation. In the modern world, this manifests as widespread social unrest, activism, and radicalization among communities that feel abandoned by the legal system. El Filibusterismo remains a vital cautionary tale, warning modern societies that failing to address institutional injustice and widening wealth gaps will inevitably lead to instability and social collapse.
